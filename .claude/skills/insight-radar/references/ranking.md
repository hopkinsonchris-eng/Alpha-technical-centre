# Ranking: evidence, readiness, impact

Two independent axes, scored separately, then combined. Keep them separate in
your head — a well-evidenced trivial finding and a badly-evidenced important one
are different problems, and collapsing them into one score hides which is which.

---

## Axis 1: evidence tier

What kind of thing is this, and how much weight will it bear? Assign the tier
from the **strongest source you have actually read**, not the strongest source
that exists.

| Tier | What it is | Test |
|---|---|---|
| **E1** | Peer-reviewed or refereed | Published in a society journal or refereed proceedings: SPE Journal, Geophysics, AAPG Bulletin, Petrophysics, Petroleum Geoscience, JPSE/GSE, or a refereed conference paper. Has a method section and stated uncertainty. |
| **E2** | Society or agency published, named authors, real data | JPT, The Leading Edge, First Break, AAPG Explorer, DOE/NETL reports, IOGP, IEA, regulator filings, a new or revised API standard. Not peer-reviewed but institutionally accountable. Also: Argus/Platts/Reuters for market and price data, where they are the primary source. |
| **E3** | Operator or vendor technical disclosure | A field trial with numbers, a technical paper from an interested party, a case study with production or cost figures. Credible detail, but the author benefits from the conclusion. |
| **E4** | Trade press reporting | Upstream, Hart, World Oil, OGJ, Rigzone reporting on someone else's work. Fine for existence and timing; weak for magnitude. |
| **E5** | Press release or marketing with no data | **Not citable.** If E5 is all there is, the item does not go in the edition. |

**Inclusion rules.**
- Every edition must rest on at least one E1 or E2 source. The script enforces this.
- The spine of the article — the developments given their own section — should be
  E1 to E3. An E4-only item can appear in the radar table with its tier shown,
  but must not be written up as though the result is established.
- One source is never enough for a headline claim. Two independent sources, or
  one E1, or say plainly that it is a single report.
- A vendor claim quoted from the vendor is attributed to the vendor in the prose,
  every time. "SLB reports a 30% reduction" — not "the technique reduces by 30%".

## Axis 2: readiness

Where is it on the path to being usable? This is what a reader actually wants to
know, and it is the number most reporting omits.

| Level | Label | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Lab / concept | Bench, synthetic data, simulation-only, or a method paper with no field application. |
| 2 | Pilot | One or a few wells, a single field trial, a proof of concept on real data. |
| 3 | Field-proven | Repeated deployment across wells, fields or basins, with published results. |
| 4 | Commercial | Available to buy or licence, in routine use, or embedded in a standard. |

Do not inflate. A method demonstrated on one synthetic dataset is level 1 even
if the paper's conclusion is enthusiastic.

---

## Axis 3: potential impact (score 1–5)

Score each factor 1–5, then take the mean and round to one decimal. Show your
working in the dossier so the ranking can be argued with.

1. **Magnitude.** How big is the claimed effect, and is it quantified?
   5 = a step change with numbers (recovery factor, cost, cycle time, accuracy).
   3 = a real but incremental gain, quantified. 1 = qualitative improvement only.
2. **Breadth.** How many assets does it touch?
   5 = most fields in most basins. 3 = a common asset class or one large basin.
   1 = a narrow niche or one unusual reservoir type.
3. **Adoption cost.** What does it take to use?
   5 = works with data and tools an operator already has.
   3 = new software, a service contract, or a modest capex item.
   1 = new facilities, a rig programme, or a multi-year data acquisition.
4. **Time to value.** How soon does it pay?
   5 = this year. 3 = next budget cycle. 1 = long-cycle, beyond five years.
5. **Workflow relevance.** Alpha Technical Centre does integrated field
   development planning, mature-field redevelopment, technical due diligence,
   nodal analysis and reservoir simulation.
   5 = directly usable in that work tomorrow. 3 = adjacent and worth knowing.
   1 = interesting but outside what clients ask for.

   Score this on the **workflow and asset class**, never the region. A
   brownfield waterflood method is a 5 whether the field is in Oman, Vietnam,
   Colombia or Oklahoma. An ultra-deepwater subsea technique is a 2 or 3
   wherever it was proven, because it is further from the work, not because of
   where it happened.

**Geography is not a ranking factor.** Do not score an item up because it came
out of Latin America or the US Gulf Coast, and do not score it down because it
came out of Norway, Kazakhstan, China or Australia. Physics and engineering
transfer between basins; the interesting question is whether the *reservoir or
facility* is analogous, not whether the flag is familiar. Where an analogue is
genuinely relevant, that belongs in the prose as context, not in the score.

**Composite rank** = impact mean, then break ties by evidence tier (E1 first),
then by readiness (higher first). Report the composite in the dossier, not in
the article — readers get the radar table, not the arithmetic.

## What earns a place in the edition

Aim for **five to eight developments**. Fewer looks thin; more and nothing gets
room to breathe.

Include:
- Anything scoring impact ≥ 3.5 at E1–E3.
- Anything at readiness 4 that changes what is commercially available, even at
  a lower impact score — availability is news.
- A genuinely important negative result. A method that was shown *not* to work,
  or a field trial that underperformed, is more valuable than a fifth
  incremental improvement, and almost nobody reports it.

Exclude:
- E5-only items, however exciting.
- Re-announcements of something already in the ledger with no new evidence.
- Company financial and M&A news. This is a technology radar, not a market
  report; the site already runs market pieces separately.
- Anything you could not verify. A development you cannot source does not exist
  for the purposes of this article.

## Balance

**Disciplines.** Across an edition, spread them: do not run six subsurface items
and nothing on facilities or commercial. Across a *year*, every discipline in the
checklist should have appeared at least once. If a discipline has been silent for
three editions, say so in that edition's dossier and go looking harder — it is
more likely a search failure than a genuinely quiet field.

**Regions.** The series is global. Check the regional spread of every edition
before publishing and record it in the dossier:

- North America, Latin America, North Sea and Europe, Middle East, Africa,
  Asia-Pacific, CIS and Caspian.
- No single region should hold more than about half an edition's items. If one
  does, that is a search failure, not a real distribution — the Middle East and
  Asia-Pacific in particular publish heavily through ADIPEC, IPTC, SPE Kuwait
  and Asia-Pacific conferences, CNPC and Petronas technical output, and the
  Chinese and Japanese society literature.
- If a region has not appeared in three editions, go looking specifically.

A field trial in Vietnam and one in Colombia carry equal weight. The only
question is the quality of the evidence and the size of the effect.

State the honest bottom line for each item. "Promising, single pilot, no
independent replication" is a more useful sentence than any amount of
enthusiasm, and it is what makes the series worth reading.
