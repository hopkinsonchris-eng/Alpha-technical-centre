# Wave 6 — Innovation Options

Each option is checked against the AI-leverage list: an LLM reasoning over the firm's own data; pattern recognition the eye would miss; optimisation of parameters set by habit; first-draft artifacts a person then edits.

## Option A — Everything in, by consent, with a cited morning account (RECOMMENDED)

The four Gate 1 choices built as one system: consent at first sign-in (D60), full capture under Protected and Blocked rules (D61), a quiet 180-day history (D62), and Send from the person's own address (D63); plus the given practices (filing memory, status per message, one record per Message-Id, bulk apart, who last spoke, transparency, WorkDrive changes, Books by modified time). The one new thing: a **What came in** card on Today that, per opportunity the person holds, says in a sentence what moved since they last looked, with a chip on every claim that opens the record.

- *LLM over the firm's data:* the overnight sentence per opportunity is written by the provider from the new messages, files and invoices filed there since the person's last visit, under the drafter's rule (every claim cites a record, or it is not said). The classifier's tie-break stays as it is.
- *Pattern recognition:* the filing memory (P51) learns from every Assign, so one partner's decision files the other four's copies; bulk detection (P56) keeps newsletters out of sight; relationship strength (P57) is recency and frequency, not an LLM.
- *Optimisation:* the filing threshold stays at 0.85, but the memory raises a thread's confidence to 1.0 once anyone filed it, which removes most of the queue without touching the bar.
- *First drafts:* the overnight sentence and, through Send, the letter that leaves from the partner's address.

Why recommended: it is the smallest build that makes the Vault the first thing opened each morning, every piece is on the practice scan with a named precedent, and the AI is used only where a deterministic rule cannot do the job (saying what the day's mail means).

## Option B — The inbox agent

Every captured message is read by the provider, which names the project, the intent (request, information, approval, chase), the deadline if any, and drafts a reply for the person to accept. The queue becomes a list of suggested replies.

- *LLM:* heavy, on every message. *Pattern:* intent and deadline extraction. *Optimisation:* none. *First drafts:* a reply per message.

Why not now: it reads every message with the model at a cost that scales with the mailbox, it moves the person from deciding to approving, and the firm has not yet seen a single week of captured mail to say which intents matter. It is the natural second step once Option A has run for a month.

## Option C — Threads as living records

Each conversation thread gets one note that the provider rewrites on every new message: what was asked, what was agreed, what is open, who owes what, with citations into the messages. The project file shows threads, not messages.

- *LLM:* one rewrite per new message in a thread. *Pattern:* the open items across threads. *Optimisation:* none. *First drafts:* the thread note.

Why not now: strong for long negotiations, but it changes what a record is (a note that supersedes itself on every message) and needs the research of its own wave on how to show a thread's history without hiding the messages. It fits after Option A, when the threads exist.

## Option D — Feeds only, no AI

Capture, filing, backfill, Send and the transparency page, with no overnight sentence; the Today card shows counts and the queue shows the rest.

- *No AI leverage:* the stated reason is that capture itself gains nothing from a model, and the deterministic signals plus the filing memory place most mail.

Why not: it leaves the person to read the day's mail to know what changed, which is the gap. It is the fallback if the overnight sentence fails its own citation rule in testing; everything else in Option A is built the same way.
