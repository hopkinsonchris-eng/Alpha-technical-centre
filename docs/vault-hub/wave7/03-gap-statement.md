# Wave 7 — Gap Statement

Tooler phase 3, 5 October 2026. Derived from the Practice Scan (P61 to P73), the four team reports and the Choice Sheet (D64 to D67).

Nobody currently gives a small upstream advisory firm a workspace that, the moment a project opens in a new country, assembles that country's law, current licence round and its dates, fiscal terms from the signed contracts, operators, regulator and data-room terms from public licensed sources with every sentence linked to a dated stored original, and then keeps each section fresh on its own cadence beside the firm's own confidential record of the same opportunity. The evidence is the scan: the upstream intelligence platforms (Wood Mackenzie Lens, Rystad Cube, S&P, GlobalData, Welligence) hold the country knowledge but are paid, closed and separate from the firm's files; the open regulator data (NSTA, Sodir, ANH, Argentina, ANP, BOEM), the contract repositories (ResourceContracts, EITI) and the free law-firm country chapters exist but nobody joins them to a project file; the research terminals (AlphaSense) deep-link every sentence to its source but only over their own corpus; and the firm's own Hub, as the snag list and the UI review show, does not yet state stage · next · last in one line, cannot make a run final, hides runs from Find and lets a document from one client surface in another client's letter. Doing it would win the two things the team cannot buy: the first morning in a new country spent on judgement rather than on Safari, and a project file a partner can open cold after a month and trust in one glance, because the public pack and the private record sit together, both cited, both dated, both honest about what they do not know. This claim is falsifiable: if, after Wave 7, an engineer opening a Namibia or Guyana project still has to look up the Act, the round deadline, the royalty or the regulator by hand, or if a partner opening a project cold cannot see its stage, next action and last activity without scrolling, the gap was not closed.
