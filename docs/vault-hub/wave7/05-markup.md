### 1.8 The country opening pack (PR4 and PR5) — P61, P62, P64, P65, P70, P71, P72, D67

BEFORE: a project opened in a new country has a country brief from the firm's own records, a World Monitor risk card and a research run; the law, the round, the fiscal terms, the operators, the regulator and the data room are looked up by hand.

AFTER: `vault/master/country-sources.json`, reviewed in pull requests: per country and per section the sources (URL or adapter), TTL, licence and attribution line, and the domain allow-list; the generic sources (Chambers and Legal 500 chapter series resolved to the current edition at build time, EITI, ResourceContracts, GEM, EIA, JODI) apply to every country; UK, Norway, Colombia, Argentina, Brazil and US offshore have regulator adapters (ArcGIS FeatureServer, CKAN, CSV); Mexico, Namibia and Guyana are seeded by hand with their regulator pages. Creating a project with a country, or "Assemble the pack" on the country panel, queues a `country-pack` job. The job fetches every source server-side with the miners' rate-limited `Http`, stores the bytes as immutable originals under project `firm` with `origin {source, external_id: url, fetched_at}` and a sha256 manifest, unchanged payloads write nothing, changed ones become a new version, and the ingest extracts and chunks the text so Find reaches the law. Each of the ten sections (legal framework; licensing and the current round; fiscal terms; who works there; the service industry; regulator and data room; production, reserves and market; risk, linking the existing card; basin literature, linking the existing miners; open questions) is drafted from its stored originals only, in short sentences each ending `[doc:<id>]`, with `checkCitations` unchanged, bilingual in one call; a section whose sources were unreachable says "no source reached"; the service-industry section says "no public register; the firm's contacts here are …" when that is the truth. `country_packs` (migration 009): country, section, version, body (sentences, citations, changed_since), source_items, built_at, model, spend, status; TTL per section (law 180 days, licensing 7, fiscal 90, companies the GEM release plus 30, service 180, regulator 180, production monthly, risk through the adapter); the nightly job marks a section stale on TTL or a changed source sha; the weekly cron rebuilds stale sections of countries with active projects under `PACK_BUDGET_GBP` (default 2 per build) and writes a "what changed" line. Presentation: a Country pack card on the project page between the opportunity card and the File card, ten rows with a headline sentence and a freshness dot (fresh, due, stale, unreachable), each opening the bottom sheet with the section, its chips (source and fetch date), what changed, and Open the original at the cited passage; the globe's country panel shows "Pack: assembled 5 Oct · 9 of 10 · 1 stale" and the button; Today shows one line when a pack lands or a section changes; `vault://countries/<cc>/pack.md` as an MCP resource and the pack headlines in `get_project_context`; the pack's originals are sources Write to… may cite. The pack is `lt-public`, built from public sources only, and never receives a client record as context; the fetch prompts carry only the country and the section's registry URLs with `allowed_domains` and `max_uses` set from the registry. Legal and fiscal sections carry "orientation for screening; verify against the instrument in force".

## 5. Risks and the rollback path

- **The scope predicate change (AC1, AC2) is the one change that could hide a record someone expects.** The rule is unchanged in intent (firm records visible firm-wide in firm scope) and narrowed only in project and client scope; the test suite's scope tests run before it merges; rollback is one commit on `gateway/scope.ts`.
- **The stateline and Today reorder change the first screen everyone sees.** PR2 ships behind nothing, so rollback is reverting the pull request; the register and globe data are untouched.
- **Migration 008 and 009 are additive.** Rollback is leaving the tables in place and reverting the code; nothing is dropped, nothing is overwritten (rule 9).
- **The pack can be wrong or stale.** Every sentence cites a dated snapshot, every section shows its dot and "as of", the legal and fiscal sections carry the orientation caveat, the service section is honest about having no register; a wrong sentence is corrected by rebuilding the section after fixing the registry, never by editing the text.
- **Sources change shape or block the server.** The registry stores series, not fixed URLs; an adapter reports "unreachable since"; the recorded fixtures keep the tests green regardless; the Chambers 2025 index already rolled over during the research.
- **Cost.** `PACK_BUDGET_GBP` per build and a monthly ceiling; spend on the cost page by feature; a country with no active project is never refreshed.
- **Licences.** ResourceContracts content stays in the public-scope pack (share-alike); IEA datasets are never fetched; OnePetro stays metadata-only; attribution lines travel on the chip and in the stored item.
- **Public pages.** CI diffs every sitemap URL against `main`; the asset change reaches the tools through the shared client library and the query string, never by editing a sitemap page.
