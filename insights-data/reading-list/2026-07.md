# Reading list, July 2026 edition

Papers that looked significant this month but could not be read without society
access, so they were **not** published. Ranked by what they are likely to be
worth to us.

Drop PDFs in `insights-data/papers/2026-08/` and the August run reads them in
full and cites the results. That folder is git-ignored, so nothing licensed gets
committed.

All of the below sit on OnePetro, which returns HTTP 403 to automated access.
The `JPT` synopsis pages are member-restricted and truncate after the
introduction.

---

## Priority 1: pull these first

**1. SPE 230613 — Closed-loop completions programme**
First closed-loop hydraulic fracturing programme, designed and field deployed.
The public portion describes three layers, sensing, decision logic and
execution, running fully autonomous fracturing and compressing decisions from
minutes or hours to seconds. Operator, basin, well count and every result are
behind the wall.
https://jpt.spe.org/closed-loop-completions-program-holds-potential-to-transform-hydraulic-fracturing-restricted
*Why:* if the gain is real this is the most consequential completions item of
the month. It was dropped from the edition purely because the results are
unreadable.

**2. OTC 36263 — Encapsulated polymer overcomes shear degradation and injectivity loss offshore**
*Why:* shear degradation and injectivity loss are the two failure modes that
have killed most offshore polymer floods. A paper claiming to address both is
worth reading whatever it concludes.

**3. "The New Brownfield of Venezuela"** — JPT June 2026 feature
https://jpt.spe.org/the-new-brownfield-of-venezuela-restricted
*Why:* directly relevant to the firm's regional work and to mature-field
redevelopment. Feature-length, so likely to carry more context than a paper.

**4. SPE 230249 — Test proves validity of in-situ combustion in the Permian Basin**
*Why:* in-situ combustion has a long record of good laboratory work and
disappointing field application. A validity test in a heavily instrumented basin
is informative whichever way it came out.

## What access would change

This edition ran to four items. Ten of the leads above are papers with results we
could not state, across completions, EOR, well integrity and coiled tubing. With
priority 1 and 2 in hand, the same month would have supported seven or eight
developments, and three of the disciplines that appear as gaps in the dossier
would have been covered.

## What not to do

Do not give the run your SPE credentials to log in with. Cloud environment
variables are readable by anyone using the environment, there is no secrets
store, and automated retrieval from a subscription database would breach SPE's
terms and put your membership at risk. A human downloading papers under their
own licence and dropping them in a folder is both safer and, at ten papers a
month, faster.
